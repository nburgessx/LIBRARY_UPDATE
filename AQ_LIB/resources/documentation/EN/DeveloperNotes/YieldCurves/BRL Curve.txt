1.
BRL curve and other emerging country currency curves are calibrated using non-deliverable forwards and swaps. They can be interpreted as a discounting curve for the home leg but in USD collateral.

2. 
The market rate for a NDF represents the implied zero rate of the home leg that together with the zero rate of the same tenor from the foreign (USD) leg, produce a forward FX rate that is equal to the NDF's FX rate, as dictated by interest rate parity.

3. 
Given a calibrated BRL curve, currently MLib can not imply a zero rate that is precisely equal to the input NDF zero rate of the same tenor. This is due to the spot df adjustment present at the end of basis curve calibration. The discrepency is 1-3 basis points.

4.
Using a calibrated BRL curve, currently MLib can not price a standard BRLUSD NDS to PV 0. The residual PV amounts to about 3 for a 3Y NDS with a USD notional of 1mio. The reason is due to different spot df adjustments being applied to the BRL leg and the USD leg during the calibration process of the BRL curve. This problem is prevalent in all non-MTM cross currency curves.
